SLS Annual Assembly 2026 · Experience design

Four hundred people in one room. The point is what they leave connected to.

The 2026 assembly is one day, all members, no graduation. That removes the ceremony that organised the last one and leaves a harder question: what does an executive society actually convene for? This is a design for the answer — the network graph you already have, running as the spine of the day, and a programme built around the two things the 2024 survey scored lowest.

What 2024 measured

Operations scored. The programme didn’t.

Overall satisfaction in 2024 was 76.8%. Underneath that number the survey splits cleanly, and it splits in a way that should decide where 2026’s attention goes. Every operational item — signage, parking, the venue, the pre-event information, registration — scored 79% or better. Both programme items scored below 75%.

The digital spine

Make the graph the day, not a kiosk in the corner

An interactive screen that people glance at on the way to coffee is a decoration. The same graph, given a job at every hour, becomes the thing members tell each other about. Seven moments, one dataset.

  • Two weeks out

    The profile is the RSVPEvery member gets a link to their own node. Two questions before they can see anyone else’s: what are you working on this year, and what could you help another member with. Confirming attendance and completing the profile are the same action — which is how the 48-hour headcount reconfirmation finally gets teeth.

  • At the door

    The badge scan lights your nodeThe wall starts almost empty, and everyone walking in can see that it does. Arriving is visibly an act of joining something, not queueing for it.

  • 15:30

    The Council opens against the wallNot a slide deck. Here is the society, tonight, in this room — who is here, from which sectors, and where it has grown since last year.

  • Through the afternoon

    The graph is the interfaceFilter by sector, by challenge, by chapter. Request an introduction and it comes back with a place and a time to meet, not a business card. Peer circle tables and dinner seating are drawn from the same data, so no table is single-sector and nobody workshops a live problem in front of their closest competitor.

  • As projects land

    Commitments become edgesEvery commitment made at the Impact Market is entered on the wall. By 19:00 the graph shows what the society built this year, not only who knows whom.

  • 19:40

    Ninety seconds, lights downThe graph replays from doors to close. Then the ledger: introductions requested, introductions completed. That number is the report to the 2027 assembly, and the first time this society has had one.

  • The week after

    Everyone leaves with their own cardTheir node, their new connections, their commitments. The graph stays live as the year-round directory — which is also what keeps the profiles current enough to run this again in 2027.

Run of show

Two hours seated out of six and a half

The single loudest note in the 2024 feedback was the sitting. This shape answers it arithmetically: 120 minutes of seated programme, 270 of moving, and the seated block runs once, unbroken, at the end — because the hardest thing 2024’s team did was get a networking crowd back into a theatre.

The five pillars, placed

Each one, treated as a format problem

The pillars are right. What decides whether they land is the format each one is given. For every pillar below: the version most assemblies default to, and the version this day runs.

Vision 2030 impact

Default: members present projects from a stage to a seated room.

Run it as an Impact Market. Twelve to fifteen member-led stands across the venue, each carrying exactly one ask — a partner, a pilot site, a regulator conversation. Members walk, talk and commit; every commitment is entered on the wall as an edge. The output is a list of commitments, not applause.

Induction of new leaders

Default: a graduation, with a procession and certificates.

There is no graduation in 2026. New members from the latest Misk Fellowship and 2030 Leaders cohorts are inducted into the society in fifteen minutes: named on the wall, paired on the spot with a sponsor member who is standing there, and the room stands once. If even that reads as ceremony the Track doesn’t want this year, cut it — nothing else in the day depends on it.

Executive knowledge exchange

Default: a keynote, then a Q&A that never comes.

Two dialogues of thirty minutes each, paired on stage, moderated by a member rather than a broadcaster, with the questions collected from members through the graph in the fortnight before. 2024’s feedback was specific about this: shorter, more inspiring, an interviewer matched to the interviewee, and leaders sharing what happened to them rather than general advice. The deeper exchange happens in the peer advisory circles, where tables are mixed by sector from the graph.

Community governance

Default: the Council presents a roadmap and invites questions.

The Council brings three decisions the membership actually makes: which international chapter opens next, which transformational visit the society funds, and one change to how members are engaged. Voted in the room, results on the wall before the segment ends. An elected body that only presents stops being one.

Relationship building

Default: a networking break, and hope.

Introductions are requested through the graph and delivered with a place and a time. Mentorship pairs are matched from the same data and must set a first meeting date before they leave the room — a pairing that ends in a handshake has evaporated by Tuesday. Dinner seating comes from the graph too, so the last seventy minutes are designed rather than left to chance.

What makes it memorable

Nine decisions, most of them free

None of these need budget so much as a decision taken early and held. Each one answers something the 2024 report already told you.

  • Put the strongest hour last. In 2024 the theatre ran long and half the audience left the moment it ended. Give people a reason to still be there at 19:40, and make the close worth staying for rather than a thank-you slide.
  • Never ask a room to reassemble. The hardest operational moment of 2024 was pulling a networking crowd back into the theatre; doors opened and closed through the whole second half. Front-load the moving segments, then run the seated block once, unbroken.
  • Dialogues, not speeches. Thirty minutes, a member moderating, questions from the membership. Brief every speaker on one rule: tell us what happened to you, not what we should do.
  • One signature image. Ninety seconds of the graph replaying with the lights down. Every event people remember has one thing they photograph — choose it deliberately instead of leaving it to the stage set.
  • Publish the guest rule with the invitation. In 2024 members asked for more than one guest each. Decide it, print it on the invitation, and stop negotiating it in the final week.
  • Digital badge, no pick-up trip. 2024 sent guests travelling in from outside Riyadh on a separate journey to collect a badge, to a location beside the venue they were coming to anyway.
  • Cater to the reconfirmed number. 2024 catered for 400 and served 150–200. Contract to a headcount reconfirmed 48 hours out, with a staged release for the rest.
  • Build the agenda around prayer, not against it. Members asked for more time at the break. Maghrib and Isha are fixed points on 21 November; put a moving segment across them so the room never has to be stopped and restarted.
  • The takeaway is the network card. Personal, near-free, and it keeps the graph in front of members for the eleven months when there is no assembly.

Open decisions

For the Track and the Council

  • Confirm 21 November and the 14:30–21:00 window against the Council diary and venue availability.
  • Decide whether the induction segment runs at all this year, given there is no graduation.
  • Guest policy: one per member, or more — and published with the invitation.
  • Confirm the Council will put three decisions to a live vote; the segment does not work as a presentation.
  • Member consent and data accuracy for the graph — profiles, sectors and contact preferences current before invitations go out.
  • Budget, cost-centre codes and named owners, all still TBC on the charter.
